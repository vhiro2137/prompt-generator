# Analysis and learning prompts

Read this reference for teaching, research, document or media analysis, interviews, and data analysis.

## Teaching

Have the target AI calibrate to the learner's stated level, connect concepts to the actual material or project, and proceed from a plain-language model to precise definitions and evidence-backed examples. For extended teaching, use lessons with objectives, prerequisite concepts, code or examples, common mistakes, practice, and comprehension checks. Do not invent the learner's experience.

## Research and document analysis

Require complete coverage of the relevant material, source priority, current verification for changing facts, locatable citations, explicit uncertainty, and a clear distinction between original requirements and suggestions. Respect copyright by summarizing and transforming rather than reproducing long passages.

## Video, audio, and interviews

Require timestamps for observed items, uncertainty markers for unclear speech, and separate labels for recorded questions versus inferred or expanded questions. For interview preparation, useful output may include original-answer critique, a direct conclusion, a spoken answer, deeper explanation, likely follow-ups, scoring criteria, and a focused study plan. Protect irrelevant personal data.

## Data analysis

Define the decision or question, data sources, schema and units, cleaning rules, missing-data treatment, metrics, calculations, uncertainty, validation, visualization, and reproducibility. Prevent unsupported causal claims and fabricated precision.

## Artifacts

When the target AI must create a document, spreadsheet, slide deck, image, site, or other artifact, specify the real output format and destination, content and visual quality criteria, appropriate format-specific verification, and delivery requirements. Do not accept a prose description when the user asked for an actual artifact.
